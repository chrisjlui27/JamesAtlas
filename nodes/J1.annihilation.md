## Law

| form | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| none | the strata of `A <A>` | a − a = 0; ln a − ln′ a ∈ Λ |

Inversion, `A <A> ≐ void`, is James's third axiom and is strict in 𝕁₁ as the chart has it. The legislation of 2026-08-22 says it should be *stratified*: void in some contexts, ≃ ω in others. Lui's guess (LEGISLATION 30d), in his words: "moving from J0 to J1 is the shift from inversion to void and inversion to period, and period gets absorbed within an instance with a receipt" — read as he said it: in 𝕁₀ annihilation goes *to void*; in 𝕁₁ it goes *to the period* — `A <A>` lands in Λ, witnessed, not strictly void — and inside an instance the period is absorbed with a receipt. (The chart's gloss on *where* that would bite — two abstracts of the same content on different sheets, so `[B] <[B]>` cancels only mod Λ — is the session's reading, not his.) The three-boundary calculus does not yet have the rule; that rule is this gap.

## Plain

A number minus itself is nothing. The log of a number minus the log of the same number is nothing too — unless the two logs went round the circle a different number of times; when they may, the calculus does not yet say.

## Derivation

none — gap.

## Confirms

That the stratification is real: in the universal cover of ℂ×, two lifts of the same point differ by a lattice element, so "the same number twice" is ambiguous exactly at the log level and nowhere else. And that it is where [[J1.pole]] lives: `[A <A>]` is the pole □ when the annihilation went to void and `[ω]`, a finite number, when it went to period — "the fork IS the object" (legislation 2026-08-22). It is **not** a defect of Inversion as an axiom of forms: `A <A>` with A one and the same form is void in every reading; the question is about two *occurrences* that the page writes alike.

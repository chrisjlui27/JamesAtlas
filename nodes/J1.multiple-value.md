## Law

| form | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| `J J J ≃ J` | three half-periods are one half-period, mod Λ — with the period ω posted | iπ + 2πi ≡ iπ (mod 2πi): the log of −1 is iπ + Λ |

James found that his calculus made J ambiguous — `J = JJJ = …` and `void = JJ = JJJJ = …` — and called it a defect. 𝕁₁ reads the same equations as true mod Λ, with the receipt saying by how much: the log of −1 is not a number but a coset, J + Λ, and the "ambiguity" is the lattice itself.

## Plain

Three half-turns and one half-turn point the same way; the difference is a whole turn, and 𝕁₁ hands you the receipt for it instead of calling the calculus broken.

## Confirms

Ln(−1) = iπ + 2πiℤ: the multivaluedness of the logarithm, stated for the one value the calculus names. James's two constraints of procedure (p. 47; [[C.james-discipline]]) were written to keep this ambiguity from being used; 𝕁₁ uses it. It is **not** `J ≐ <J>` (James's other symptom, [[C.phase-sign]]): `J <J>` is void strictly by Inversion and `J J` is ω, so J and `<J>` differ by ω on the page — J ≃ <J> mod Λ, exactly as James saw, and not strictly. The coset structure: Λ = ℤω ⊂ ½Λ = ℤJ, with ½Λ/Λ = ℤ/2 = {unity, negation} — the group and the torsor.

## Law

| statement | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| inside: ✠, `(A □) ≡ void` strict; outside: Ω, −∞ + Λ, one class; a receipt at the pole is indeterminate | — | the one value no instance can be is 0; the log of 0 has every branch |

What □ is in 𝕁₁ (LEGISLATION 30f). The three classical laws of the black hole stand — `(□) ≐ void`, `(A □) ≐ void`, `□ A ≐ □` — and the two exclusions with them; what changes is the reading of the empty square *from outside*. Dominion `□ ω ≡ □` would, with □ a value, forget a lattice element without a receipt, against L0. With □ the pole it forgets nothing determinate: ln 0 has every branch, so the pole is already the whole coset −∞ + Λ, and absorbing a period into it changes nothing. A receipt whose content evaluates to the pole has no value — the prover shows `k = ?` — and is never cancelled.

## Plain

Zero's logarithm is minus infinity, and minus infinity plus a whole turn is still minus infinity: the pole does not keep count. From inside a circle it is simply nothing; from outside it is everything the lattice could be at once.

## Confirms

The page-logic reading of the empty square (✠ and Ω, concession and divergence, from ludics) carried back to the arithmetic. It is **not** a change of law: the same form, the same three laws, the same two exclusions. It is a change in what the chart *says* about the empty square from outside — and the one reading under which L0 needs no exemption at the pole.

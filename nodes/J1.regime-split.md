## Law

| statement | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| no angle, no `[()]`, no `[[…]]` ⟹ every receipt void; each door alone lets Λ in | — | the local sector ℝ₊ has a single-valued log; U(1) does not |

The July consolidation's T4 said the angle-free fragment is Λ-free: phase is reachable only through `< >`. The witness refutes that as stated and finds the true statement: there are **three doors**. The angle (−1 has log iπ); the count of one (`[()]` is ω, by the very definition of 𝕁₁); and a square directly inside a square (ln a is negative for a < 1, so ln ln a carries iπ with no mirror anywhere — the signature failure of [[mult.seam-signature]], `[[[oo]]]`). Close all three and every unit step on the trunk's positive arithmetic — addition, multiplication, roots — has a void receipt: that is the local sector, ℝ₊, where 𝕁₁ and the classical calculus agree to the letter. Exponentiation `(([[A]][B]))` nests squares and is the first place in positive arithmetic where the lattice *can* enter by the third door; on the trunk the first non-void receipts come earlier and by the other two — the log of one ([[mult.log-of-one]], the count of one) and the mirrors of ℚ (`Q.reciprocal-of-product`, `Q.reciprocal-of-negative`, the angle) — and the receipts of [[alg.exp-law]] come from its complex domain, not from its squares.

## Plain

Add, multiply and take roots of positive numbers and you never need a receipt. Three things open the door to the lattice: a minus sign, the log of one, and taking the log of a log.

## Confirms

The local/colocal split of the July set, made exact: the Λ-free sector is not "angle-free" but "angle-free, ω-free and without nested squares" — James's canonical numeral forms, products and roots of positives. It is **not** a statement about values alone: `[[[oo]]]` *has* a positive real under every square and still reaches the lattice, because a log of a positive real can be negative. And it is **not** the ψ-wall: which fractions of ω survive is a further selection ([[J1.psi-wall]]); this only says where fractions of ω can arise.

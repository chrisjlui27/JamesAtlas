## Law

| form | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| `(ω) ≡ ()` | the instance of the period form is the empty instance | e^{2πi} = 1 |

The period axiom of the July consolidation turns out to be a theorem, and a strict one: ω is `[()]`, so `(ω)` is `([()])`, the instance of an abstract — and the counit is strict. Bott periodicity, in boundary arithmetic, is the counit at one.

## Plain

Go round once and take the instance: you are at one. The calculus does not even need a receipt for this — the circle around the box is the strict half of Involution.

## Confirms

e^{2πi} = 1. In the July axiom set this was A5, with ω ≠ void adjoined beside it and the whole justified by confluence; with receipts as 2-cells the equation itself is the strict counit at `()` and needs no justification — what the confluence argument was really forcing is absorption, [[J1.absorption]], which *does* carry a receipt. It is **not** the statement that ω is void: `(ω) ≡ ()` identifies the instances, and the abstracts `[()]` = ω and `[(void)]`… are the same form — ω — which 𝕁₁ does not reduce. Instances forget the winding; abstracts keep it.

## Law

| statement | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| the page has two ends on the count of `[p]` and admits one: the pole, past the depth; the reciprocals of the prime, below zero, are excluded. At ∞ the same two ends are −∞ (the pole) and +∞ (divergent) | — | ℤ_p ∪ {0} versus ℚ_p: the one-point and the two-point ends of the valuation |

[[J1.pole]] declared the empty square polarity-dependent at ∞ — ✠ inside an instance, Ω outside — and owed the place calculus its own pole structure. Here it is. The log space at a finite place is a line of counts of `[p]`, and the line has two ends. At the top, past the depth, is the **pole**: a count of `[p]` that is the log of the void pile ([[place.pole]]), whose instance is void — the black hole with the three laws of [[mult.black-hole]]. At the bottom, below zero, is the **other end**: the negative counts of `[p]`, whose instances would be the reciprocals of the prime and are not forms ([[place.no-inverse-of-the-prime]]) — the mirror of the pole, which [[mult.hole-exclusion]] excludes at every place. At ∞ the ends are exchanged: the pole is −∞ (the instance of −∞ is 0) and +∞ diverges. A form that houses both ends is a **wheel** (the JP ledger's ⊥ = □₋□₊), and it is not drawn: it would cost total cancellation, the collapse the exclusions prevent ([[mult.consistency]]). The page is one-ended.

## Plain

A number line at a finite place has a top you can reach — nothing — and a bottom you cannot: dividing by p forever. At ∞ it is the other way round. No page gets both ends; that would be a wheel.

## Derivation

none — a declaration, the place's half of [[J1.pole]]. The two ends are already on the chart: the pole as [[place.pole]], the excluded end as [[place.no-inverse-of-the-prime]]; what this node declares is that they are the two ends of one axis, and that the ban on the wheel is kept.

## Confirms

The projective line's two points at the valuation: ℤ_p has the pole and excludes 1/p; ℚ_p has both, and is the wheel's *value* reading — the field in which the page's one-endedness is given up for inverses. It is **not** a claim that ℚ_p is forbidden: it is a claim that it is not this page, and that the decree admitting the other end — a lens, or a second page — is owed ([[place.completion]]).

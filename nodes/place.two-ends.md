## Law

| statement | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| the count of `[p]` has two ends and the page admits one: upward, the pole — the log of nothing, beyond every depth, which no finite count is; downward, the negative counts, whose instances (the reciprocals of the prime) are not forms. At ∞ the same two ends are −∞ (the pole) and +∞ (divergent) | — | ℤ_p ∪ {0} versus ℚ_p: the one-point and the two-point ends of the valuation |

[[J1.pole]] declared the empty square polarity-dependent at ∞ — ✠ inside an instance, Ω outside — and owed the place calculus its own pole structure. Here it is. The log space at a finite place has an axis of counts of `[p]`, and the axis has two ends. Upward is the **pole**: the log of nothing, □, the black hole with the three laws of [[mult.black-hole]]. No finite count of `[p]` is the pole: n·[p] is a finite log whose *instance* is the void pile at depth n ([[place.prime-power]]), and at depth n + 1 the same instance is pⁿ, not void. The pole is the limit end — where the counts go as the depth grows without bound, the tower the chart does not yet have ([[place.completion]]). Downward are the **negative counts**: legitimate logs — `<[p]>` is the mirror of a log, and `[([A] <[p]>)]` reads it — whose instances would be the reciprocals of the prime and are not forms ([[place.no-inverse-of-the-prime]]): the excluded end, the mirror of the pole, which [[mult.hole-exclusion]] excludes at every place. At ∞ the ends are exchanged: the pole is −∞ (the instance of −∞ is 0) and +∞ diverges. A form that houses both ends is a **wheel** (the JP ledger's ⊥ = □₋□₊), and it is not drawn: it would cost total cancellation, the collapse the exclusions prevent ([[mult.consistency]]). The page is one-ended.

## Plain

A number line at a finite place has a top you approach but never reach (nothing) and a bottom you cannot go below (dividing by p). At ∞ it is the other way round. No page gets both ends; that would be a wheel.

## Derivation

none — a declaration, the place's half of [[J1.pole]]. The two ends are already on the chart: the upper end as the limit of [[place.prime-power]] (the instance of n·[p] void at depth n, for every n), the lower end as [[place.no-inverse-of-the-prime]]; what this node declares is that they are the two ends of one axis, that the pole is the limit and not a finite count, and that the ban on the wheel is kept. The first draft said "past the depth the count *is* the pole, □₊ = N·[p]"; the referee's chain (2026-10-06) refuted that — a finite count that is the pole collapses the page — and the decree now places the pole beyond every depth.

## Confirms

The projective line's two points at the valuation: ℤ_p has the pole (as the limit of pⁿ → 0) and excludes 1/p; ℚ_p has both, and is the wheel's *value* reading — the field in which the page's one-endedness is given up for inverses. It is **not** a claim that ℚ_p is forbidden: it is a claim that it is not this page, and that the decree admitting the other end — a lens, or a second page — is owed ([[place.completion]]). It is **not** a claim that a finite count of `[p]` is ever the pole: ~~"finite N·[p] = □ in ℤ/p^N"~~ (the JP ledger's rung-count axis, 2026-09-13) is refuted — see [[place.prime-power]].

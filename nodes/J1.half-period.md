## Law

| form | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| `J J ≃ ω` | two half-periods are a period — on the bare page, up to the receipt the comparison costs | iπ + iπ = 2πi |

James's Theorem 2 says `J J ≐ void`; 𝕁₁ says `J J ≃ ω`, with ω ≃ void mod Λ. Both are "two half-turns make a whole turn"; the difference is whether the whole turn is nothing or something. James's own proof (p. 45) runs through the phase operator — `[<o>][<o>] = [<([<o>])>] = [<<o>>] = [o] = void` — i.e. through Theorem 1; the chart's route goes through the instance, where J J is a product of two −1's, with the one bare unit step at depth 0 posting its receipt, and ends at `[o]` where James's does — the one place the two differ is whether `[o]` is erased.

## Plain

Half a turn twice is a whole turn. James concluded "so it is nothing"; 𝕁₁ concludes "so it is ω" — and keeps the receipt that says the two conclusions differ by exactly one turn.

## Confirms

2J = ω: the log of −1 is half the period, iπ = 2πi/2; and the two −1's of the chart — the additive `<o>`, of infinite order, and the multiplicative `(J)` = −1, of order 2 — are reconciled by it: `(J)(J)`… is `(J J)` ≃ `()` ([[J1.j-cancellation]]) while `J J` itself is not void but ω. It is **not** strict: row 2 is a unit step at depth 0, witnessed by position, and its receipt stays on the tape (holds-mod-Λ) even though its value is void — in the classical calculus the same row is the unit step the seam decree *refuses* ([[C.double-phase]], [[C.james-discipline]]), the crack; the period survives here because `[o]` is never erased. Here nothing is refused and nothing collapses, because `J J ≃ ω` and `ω ≃ void` are statements mod Λ, and mod Λ they are true.

## Law

| form | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| `(J J) ≃ ()` | inside an instance, two half-turns are nothing | e^{2πi} = 1; (−1)(−1) = 1 |

What survives of James's Theorem 2: not `J J ≐ void` but its instance. Inside the instance the two periods are absorbed, so the equation is exact in value and costs two receipts the instance pays. This is the law that the classical chart keeps as [[C.period-absorption]] `(A J J) ≐ (A)`, with the seam decree admitting its seam step by position.

## Plain

Turn half way round twice and take the instance: you are at one. The whole turn is paid for inside the circle.

## Confirms

(−1)² = 1 and e^{2πi} = 1 in one law. Beside [[J1.half-period]] it is the whole story of James's Theorem 2: on the page `J J ≃ ω` (a period, witnessed), inside an instance `(J J) ≃ ()` (nothing, paid). It is **not** `J J ≐ void`, and the two receipts the instance pays are the exact cost of the difference. The two −1's: `<o>` is additive and of infinite order (`<o> <o>` is −2); `(J)` is multiplicative and of order 2 (this law); they are reconciled by 2J = ω and `(ω) ≡ ()`, never identified.

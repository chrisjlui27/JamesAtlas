## Law

| form | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| `([(([ω] <[o^{n}]>))]^{n}) ≃ o` | the n-th power of the instance of ω/n is one | (e^{2πi/n})ⁿ = 1 |

The classical chart reaches the n-th roots of unity as fractions of J, the half-period ([[schema.roots-of-unity]]: `(([J] [2k] <[n]>))`). 𝕁₁ reaches them as fractions of ω — and this is the law that [[alg.one-to-power]] becomes: 1^{1/n} is not 1 but the instance of a fraction of the period, because the value 1 has a lattice of logs. The fractional counts of ω generate ℚω/ℤω = ℚ/ℤ — the *receptacle*, the torsion of the circle.

## Plain

Cut a whole turn into n equal parts and take one; do it n times and you have gone round once, which the instance forgets. The n-th roots of 1 are the fractions of the period.

## Confirms

ζₙ = e^{2πi/n} satisfies ζₙⁿ = 1, for every n in the range. Beside [[schema.roots-of-unity]] it says that the two routes to the roots of unity — fractions of J and fractions of ω — agree, as they must, since 2J ≃ ω. It is **not** the statement that these are *all* the n-th roots of unity, nor that they are distinct (both need an order or a count of forms, [[N.order]]); and it is **not** yet the selection of which ω/n are *well formed* at a given level — that is the ψ-wall, [[J1.psi-wall]], the first place the chart's arithmetic becomes the arithmetic of the sphere.

## Law

| statement | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| M(2k) = denom(B_{2k}/4k) = |im J ⊂ π_{4k−1}ˢ| | the wall's census is the Bernoulli denominator, which is the order of im J | Adams; von Staudt–Clausen |

The one import of the rung, stated as such: that the numbers the wall produces natively ([[J1.wall-census]]) are the denominators of B_{2k}/4k, and that those are the orders of the image of J. Both are theorems of ordinary mathematics (von Staudt–Clausen for the denominators; Adams, with the Adams conjecture proved by Quillen and Sullivan, for im J); the chart does not derive them, it borrows them, and the witness checks the three numbers with exact rationals.

## Plain

The numbers the wall lets through are the same numbers that appear under the Bernoulli numbers, which are the same numbers that count the first families of maps between spheres. That the three agree is borrowed, not proved here.

## Derivation

none — adjoined, as an import, on top of the native census [[J1.wall-census]]. The witness computes B₂, B₄, B₆ exactly (Akiyama–Tanigawa) and checks denom(B_{2k}/4k) = M(2k) for k = 1, 2, 3.

## Confirms

The chart's first contact with the sphere spectrum: the torsion of the height-one formal group — the fractions of ω that pass the wall — is counted by the e-invariant. It is **not** native: the equality of the two counts is imported, and the chart says so in its price. And it is **not** the whole of im J: the orders, not the groups, and not the maps.

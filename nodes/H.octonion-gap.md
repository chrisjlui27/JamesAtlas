## Law

| form | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| none | the product of the octonions, and the question whether `[x ; y]` can be found rather than adjoined | (ab)c ≠ a(bc); the Moufang identities |

Two gaps, stated together because the second is what the first would answer. **(1) The octonions.** The rung's device, the ordered square ([[H.ordered-bracket]]), reads as the log of an ordered product, and its bookkeeping axiom splice ([[H.splice]]) says that `[A ; ([B ; C])]` and `[([A ; B]) ; C]` are one form `[A ; B ; C]` — associativity, as syntax. The octonions are the next Cayley–Dickson double and are not associative; splice fails for them (JH OP-3), and with it ordered cancellation at every position ([[H.ordered-cancellation]], `guarded` at JO) and the whole flat-list calculus. What survives is the alternative laws — the subalgebra generated by any two elements is associative, hence a quaternion algebra, where the rung's derivations run unchanged — and composition: the eight-square identity ([[H.four-squares]] one rung on). A JO setting therefore needs a depth that is *tree-shaped*, not list-shaped: a square whose entries are nested in a binary tree, with the alternative laws as the only re-bracketing. The chart has no such boundary. **(2) Emergent order.** The ordered square was *imported* — adjoined as a primitive with its axioms — where J was *found* (`[<o>]` was already a form). JH OP-5 asks whether order can emerge from the three boundaries by some tagging or re-entry trick, and the report's answer so far is no: the medium is unordered ([[void.space]]) and nothing inside it distinguishes `[A][B]` from `[B][A]` ([[H.no-go]]). The chart records the price honestly: the fourth boundary is the rung's import, and the question of its necessity is open.

## Plain

Quaternions multiply in order; octonions multiply in order but the grouping matters too, and the chart's new box has no way to say which grouping. Also: the new box was brought in from outside, and nobody yet knows whether it could have been found inside.

## Confirms

That the chart's ladder has a top on this branch: Hurwitz's theorem (the composition algebras are ℝ, ℂ, ℍ, 𝕆) says that the doubling after the octonions loses composition, so the branch ends at JO whether or not JO is laid; and that the import is priced — every H law's price includes [[H.ordered-bracket]] and its axioms, so a reader can see what the rung bought. It is **not** a gap in the quaternions: everything the rung states about ℍ is derived or witnessed; the gap is one rung on and one question under.

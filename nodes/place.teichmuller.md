## Law

| form | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| `(([[(([ω] <[o^{p-1}]>))]] [o^{p}])) ≃ (([ω] <[o^{p-1}]>))` | the instance of ω/(p−1), raised to the p-th power, is itself | τ(g)^p = τ(g): the Teichmüller representative is Frobenius-fixed |

The period at the place p is ω_p = (p−1)·J_p ([[place.reading]]), so the fraction ω/(p−1) is J_p itself, the torsion log, and its instance is the **Teichmüller element** τ — a (p−1)-th root of unity, the generator the reading chose ([[place.torsion]]). This law says what makes it torsion: the p-th power fixes it. The derivation is the wall's: p·(ω/(p−1)) is ω/(p−1) plus one whole period, and the instance absorbs the period ([[J1.absorption]]). So at an odd place the roots of unity are *exactly* the fixed points of the cardinality power ψ^p = `(([[A]] [o^{p}]))` — the Frobenius — which is how the ψ-wall ([[J1.psi-wall]]) looks from a finite place: ψ^r acts on the fractions of the period, and at odd p the fixed points of ψ^p are the whole torsion (at p = 2 the torsion ±1 is not Frobenius-fixed: ψ² sends −1 to 1 — see the refuted reading).

## Plain

Cut the whole turn of the place into p − 1 equal parts and take one; raise it to the p-th power and you get the same part back. The roots of unity at p are the things the p-th power leaves alone.

## Confirms

The Teichmüller lift and the structure ℤ_p^× = μ_{p−1} × (1 + pℤ_p): the torsion of the units is the set of Frobenius-fixed points, and it is generated by the instance of J_p. Beside [[J1.receptacle]] (every fraction of the period, instanced, is a root of unity) it says which fractions survive at p: those whose power p is themselves — the selection rule in its native form ([[place.selection]]). It is **not** the p-adic *log* of τ being J_p: the log of a root of unity converges to 0 in ℚ_p; J_p is the formal count the reading keeps, which is why J "survives only formally, as a pure phase" at p (JP ledger).

## Notes

The schema runs at the odd places 3, 5, 7; at p = 2 the torsion is ±1 = `<o>` and ω_2 = J J: the (p−1)-th fraction of the period is the period itself, and the law is the closure. The law holds at ∞ too — the engine reads each instance at its own p, so the J1 cell is by hand: the same derivation, with ∞'s period. What is the place's and not ∞'s is the *content* of the torsion: at ∞ every root of unity is Frobenius-fixed for its own prime, at p only the (p−1)-th roots exist to be fixed ([[place.selection]]).

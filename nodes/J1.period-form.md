## Law

| form | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| `ω := [()]` | the abstract of the empty instance, kept rather than erased | ω = 2πi; Λ = ℤω = {…, −2πi, 0, 2πi, 4πi, …} |

The one new object of 𝕁₁, and it is not a new boundary: it is the smallest form with a square in it, `[()]`, which the classical calculus erases ([[mult.log-of-one]]: `[o] ≐ void`) and 𝕁₁ keeps. Its instance is one — `(ω) ≡ ()` by the counit — so in the ℂ reading it is a log of 1 that is not 0: the period 2πi, the unit winding. No strict rule of 𝕁₁ erases it (ω ≃ void does hold — with the receipt ω, which is the point). Its collections `ω ω …` and their mirrors are the lattice Λ = ℤω.

## Plain

Walk once around the circle and you are back where you started — but 𝕁₁ remembers that you walked. ω is the form that remembers.

## Confirms

ω = 2πi as the generator of the period lattice of exp: e^{ω} = 1 and no smaller nonzero multiple of it has that property. The hinge of the whole enriched program: ln 1 has every branch 2πik, and the classical calculus chose k = 0 by erasing `[()]`; 𝕁₁ chooses to *name* the branch instead. It is **not** a new axiom: no equation is adjoined here; a strict rule has been withdrawn, which is why ω is irreducible by strict rules. It is **not** the half-period: J = `[<()>]` = iπ is the log of −1, half of ω ([[J1.half-period]]), and the two differ as a group differs from its torsor — unity has an identity, negation has none.

## Notes

The reading convention is a choice of lift: any 2πik with k ≠ 0 would do as the value of ω, and k = 1 is the generator. The reading is compositional (LEGISLATION 30b as amended): the log of *exactly one* — `[()]`, `[(ω)]`, `[(J J)]` alike — is 2πi, every other log principal; the engine uses it for nodes in `setting: J1` and for every receipt on every card, while the trunk's own numeric checks keep ln 1 = 0. That is why [[mult.log-of-one]]'s 𝕁₁ tape shows the receipt ω, and why `[(J J)] → J J` shows none.

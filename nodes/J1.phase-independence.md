## Law

| form | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| `[<(A)>] ≃ A J` | the abstract of the mirror of an instance is the content beside a half-period — mod Λ, for every complex A | ln(−e^a) = a + iπ + 2πik |

James stated phase independence for all A and proved it with the strict unit; the chart's classical version ([[C.phase-independence]]) had to restrict A to the reals and guard the last row by content. In 𝕁₁ the theorem is James's again — for *every* complex A — because the last row posts its receipt instead of needing a guard. The result lands in the coset J + Λ: negation is a torsor over the lattice, with J as base point.

## Plain

Box the mirror of a circled number, and the number steps outside with a half-turn beside it — for any number at all, as long as you keep the receipt for the whole turns.

## Confirms

Ln(−e^a) ≡ a + iπ (mod 2πi) for every complex a — the statement that is actually true, with the actual remainder. The classical chart located the crack in this row (its content is 2πi at A = J, and the strict step would prove `J J ≐ void`); here the same row at A = J posts the receipt ω and proves `[<(J)>] ≃ J J`, i.e. `[<<o>>]`… `[o] ≃ J J`, which is [[J1.half-period]] read backwards. It is **not** strict, and the posted receipt is exactly the information the classical setting had to legislate about. The group/torsor picture: `[()]` = ω generates the lattice (unity, with an identity); `[<()>]` = J is a base point of the coset J + Λ (negation, with none).

## Law

| statement | units | standard |
|---|---|---|
| M(2) = 24, M(4) = 240, M(6) = 504 | the ψ-wall's largest survivor at each of the first three levels | |im J| in π₃, π₇, π₁₁ |

The census of the wall, computed from the decree alone: for each level k, which n pass — n | r^{2k} − 1 for every r coprime to n — and which is the largest. The witness does it twice: by brute force on the native statement (every coprime r, every n up to 3000) and by Carmichael's function (λ(n) | 2k), and the two agree. The numbers are the ones the sphere knows: 24 = |π₃ˢ|, 240 = |im J ⊂ π₇ˢ|, 504 = |im J ⊂ π₁₁ˢ|.

## Plain

Count the allowed slices at each level and the biggest allowed denominators are 24, then 240, then 504 — the same numbers that count the first three families of maps between spheres.

## Notes

The witness's bound (n < 3000) is a computation, not a proof that no larger n passes; for the λ-criterion the maximum is a finite problem (λ(n) | 2k bounds n), and the brute-force agreement is the check that the native statement and the criterion are the same.
